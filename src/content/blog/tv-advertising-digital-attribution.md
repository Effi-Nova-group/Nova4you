For most of its history, television advertising ran on faith. Brands knew a prime-time spot reached millions of people and could feel the lift in awareness, but connecting a specific ad to a specific outcome was guesswork. That gap made TV an easy target whenever budgets tightened and digital channels showed up with clean, clickable dashboards. The irony is that the same digital signals once used to argue against TV are now the strongest evidence for it.

## The Measurement Gap That Hurt TV

Digital channels won the budget conversation because they were easy to measure, not because they always performed better. A click is simple to count. The moment a viewer sees a commercial, remembers the brand and searches for it two days later is not. When the only metrics on the table were last-click conversions, broadcast looked expensive and vague.

## How Attribution Closes the Loop

Modern attribution links broadcast airings to the digital behaviour they trigger:

- **Search and site-traffic lift**: measuring spikes in branded search and direct site visits in the minutes after a spot airs, matched against the exact airing schedule.
- **Geo-based testing**: running TV in some regions and holding it back in others, then comparing sales and lead volume across markets.
- **Connected TV and BVOD data**: using broadcaster video-on-demand platforms to reach viewers on smart TVs with the same targeting and reporting expected from digital.
- **Marketing mix modelling**: statistically separating the contribution of TV from every other channel over time, including the long-tail brand effect that click-based models miss.

### What the Data Usually Shows

When brands measure properly, TV tends to make every other channel work harder. Paid search gets cheaper because more people search for the brand by name. Social ads convert better because the audience already recognises the logo. TV's real return often sits inside other channels' results, which is exactly why last-click reporting undervalued it.

## Building a Measurable TV Campaign

Making broadcast accountable starts before the first spot goes to air:

- **Set a baseline**: capture normal search, traffic and sales patterns before launch so lift is visible.
- **Plan airings with measurement in mind**: spread spots across dayparts and regions to create clean comparison windows.
- **Give viewers a digital next step**: a memorable URL, search term or QR code makes the response easy to trace.
- **Connect TV and digital teams**: when the same people plan both, retargeting and search bids can respond in real time to broadcast moments.

## The Bottom Line

TV is not the unaccountable channel it used to be. Paired with the right tracking, it is one of the most powerful tools for building brand authority and lifting performance everywhere else. The brands that win over the next few years won't choose between broadcast and digital. They'll measure them together.
